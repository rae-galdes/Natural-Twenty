Hero of the Hills & Valleys

[Verse 1]
I met him by the roadside fair,
His eyes like coals in flame,
Skin of embers, horns of steel,
A soul marked deep by chains.

He stood and watched the creeping dark,
As one who knows its tread,
His hands had broke the slaver’s hold,
And paid the price in dead.


[Chorus 1]
In all the hills and all the valleys,
The Tiefling hearts were strong,
They stood when chains were writ as law,
And nights stretched grim and long,

Through blood-soaked roads and shattered gates,
Where right and wrong were gone,
In all the hills and all the valleys,
The Tiefling hearts were strong.


[Verse 2]
They sold his kin for sword and salt,
For banners yet to fly,
They dulled their horns and took their names
So none would hear them cry.

He learned their roads, their ledgers kept,
Their keys, their guards, their lies,
And every lock he left undone
Still haunts the dawn-lit skies.


[Chorus 2]
In all the hills and all the valleys,
The Tiefling hearts were strong,
They bled beneath the endless trade,
Yet stood against it long,

From whispered prayers to open doors,
From silence into song,
In all the hills and all the valleys,
The Tiefling hearts were strong.


[Verse 3]
I kept my strings and tankard close,
And watched the tale take breath,
While ghosts of chains still walked his words,
And freedom spoke of death.

No crown he took, no title claimed,
No Lord could pay his due,
He left his mark in broken chains
And vanished from our view.


[Chorus 3]
In all the hills and all the valleys,
The Tiefling hearts were strong,
Not beasts, nor goods, nor devil-spawn,
But folk who bore the wrong,

If freedom came in blood and ash,
It still to them belonged,
In all the hills and all the valleys,
The Tiefling hearts were strong.


[Outro]
So drink to him whose name is lost,
And those who were not saved,
For some men carry broken worlds
So others shan’t be slaves.

And if this song be sung tonight
When fires burn down low,
Remember chains are never gone
When silence still is owed.
